How it works

How a ointment manufacturing plant works

An ointment manufacturing plant is a closed set of stainless-steel vessels that melts, mixes, emulsifies and de-aerates semi-solid products — ointments, creams, gels, lotions and pastes — and then transfers the finished batch to storage or filling.

Where it is used

Pharmaceutical ointments & creamsMedicated gelsCosmetic creams & lotionsPastes

  1. Wax / oil phase

    Waxes, oils and oil-soluble ingredients are melted and mixed in the jacketed wax vessel.

  2. Water phase

    Water and water-soluble ingredients are heated and dissolved in the water vessel.

  3. Vacuum transfer

    Both phases are pulled into the main vessel by vacuum, so nothing is poured or handled manually.

  4. Mix & homogenise

    An anchor agitator with scrapers keeps product moving off the walls while the homogenizer creates a smooth, uniform emulsion.

  5. Heat, cool & de-aerate

    The jacket heats and cools the batch to the recipe temperature, and vacuum removes trapped air bubbles.

  6. Discharge & clean

    The finished batch is transferred for storage or filling, and the plant is cleaned through its CIP & SIP connections.

Buying guide

What to decide before you order

Have these answers ready and we can quote the right configuration first time.

  • Batch size per shift — the listed plants cover 300–1000 kg
  • Contact-part material: SS 316 L for product contact, SS 304 for non-contact parts
  • Homogenizer position: top, side or bottom entry
  • Heating & cooling method and temperature control
  • Automation level: manual, semi-automatic or PLC
  • Cleaning: CIP / SIP connections and hydraulic lid lifting
